# Shape Analytics: User Manual & Feature Guide

Welcome to the **Shape Analytics** module! This 3D visualization tool allows you to explore the stress and strain tensor fields generated by localized arbitrary traction vectors on basic geometries (Cuboid, Cylinder, Sphere).

This guide walks you through the UI layout, the physics capabilities, and how to interpret the heatmaps and analytics.

---

## 1. Interface Layout Overview

The application features a modern, split-view interface designed for "Industrial Precision". 

* **Left Panel (Permanent 3D Viewport):** Displays the selected 3D geometry in real-time. You can seamlessly rotate, pan, and zoom around the object to inspect the stress heatmaps from any angle.
* **Right Panel (Tabs):** Switches between the **Geometry & Load** configuration and the **Section Analytics** plots.

## 2. Geometry & Load Tab

### 2.1 Geometry Configuration
* **Shape Selector:** Choose between a `Cuboid`, `Cylinder`, or `Sphere`.
* **Dynamic Dimensions:** Based on your chosen shape, specific sliders will appear (e.g., Length/Width/Height for the Cuboid, Radius/Height for the Cylinder). The mesh instantly updates in the 3D viewport.

### 2.2 Load Application
This section determines how the force is applied to the body. We utilize Cauchy's Stress Theorem to map macroscopic forces to local traction vectors.

* **Force Magnitude:** Adjust the total force applied (in kN).
* **Patch Size (Radius):** Defines the footprint of the load. A smaller patch concentrates the stress, while a larger patch distributes it.
* **Traction Direction (2 Cases):**
  1. **Along Surface Normal (Pressure):** The force is directed perpendicular to the surface at the load patch (pure compressive stress). The red visualization arrow will point straight down into the material.
  2. **Arbitrary Vector (Uniform Shear+Normal):** Specify exact $X, Y, Z$ vector components. The force is applied at this angle uniformly across the patch, inducing complex combined shear and normal stresses.

## 3. Visualization & Deformation

### 3.1 Display Map
Select which component of the tensor field to map onto the 3D geometry:
* **Normal Stress ($\sigma_n$):** Visualizes the direct compressive/tensile stress beneath the load.
* **Max Shear Stress ($\tau_{\max}$):** Highlights regions prone to slip or yielding, derived from the principal stresses.
* **Von Mises Stress ($\sigma_v$):** The standard yield criterion for ductile materials, combining all stress components into a single equivalent value.
* **Strain Magnitude ($|\epsilon|$):** Maps the resulting physical deformation.

### 3.2 Physical Deformation
When a load is applied, the 3D mesh actually deforms! The vertices are displaced using the analytical **Boussinesq** displacement equations ($u_z$ and $u_r$).
* **Deformation Exaggeration Slider:** Real-world deformations in steel or concrete are microscopic. Use this slider (1x to 50x) to scale the displacement vectors so the physical bulging or indenting becomes clearly visible to the naked eye.

---

## 4. Section Analytics Tab

Because the 3D viewport is permanently fixed on the left, you can seamlessly open the **Section Analytics** tab on the right to view 2D plots without losing sight of the 3D model.

* **Slice Plane & Position:** (Under construction/integration) Define an intersecting plane ($XY$, $XZ$, $YZ$) through the geometry.
* **Plotly Distributions:** The graphs plot the exact magnitude of the Normal Stress ($\sigma_z$) and Shear Stress ($\tau_{rz}$) as a function of radial distance from the load center at a specific depth.

## 5. Camera Controls (OrbitControls)

The 3D viewport operates just like standard CAD software (e.g., Blender, SolidWorks):
* **Rotate:** Left-click and drag.
* **Pan:** Right-click and drag (or Shift + Left-click).
* **Zoom:** Scroll wheel.

*Tip: If the deformation is extreme and the object moves out of frame, you can easily pan the camera to follow it.*
