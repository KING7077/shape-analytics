\section{Stress at a point}
\physics{On a cut with unit normal $\mathbf n$ the traction is given by Cauchy's formula, and splits into normal and shear parts:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf t_n=\tens\sigma\,\mathbf n,\qquad
\sigma_{nn}=\mathbf n\cdot\tens\sigma\mathbf n,\qquad
\tau^2=\|\mathbf t_n\|^2-\sigma_{nn}^2 .
\end{equation}
Principal planes carry no shear, which gives the eigenproblem $\tens\sigma\mathbf n=\lambda\mathbf n$ with characteristic equation $\lambda^3-I_1\lambda^2+I_2\lambda-I_3=0$, where $I_1=\mathrm{tr}\,\tens\sigma$, $I_2=\tfrac12[(\mathrm{tr}\,\tens\sigma)^2-\mathrm{tr}(\tens\sigma^2)]$ and $I_3=\det\tens\sigma$. The roots $\sigma_1\ge\sigma_2\ge\sigma_3$ are real because $\tens\sigma$ is symmetric, and $\tau_{\max}=(\sigma_1-\sigma_3)/2$. Two equivalent stresses compare a general state with a tension test:
\begin{equation}
\sigma_{\text{Tresca}}=\sigma_1-\sigma_3,\qquad
\sigma_{vM}=\sqrt{\tfrac12\big[(\sigma_1-\sigma_2)^2+(\sigma_2-\sigma_3)^2+(\sigma_3-\sigma_1)^2\big]}=\sqrt{3J_2}.
\end{equation}
The von Mises stress depends only on the deviator, so it vanishes in hydrostatic compression.}

\code{The server (\texttt{/api/traction}, \texttt{/api/principal}) transcribes these formulas and uses \texttt{numpy.linalg.eigh}, which also returns principal directions. The browser needs principal stresses at tens of thousands of vertices per update, so \file{physics.js} uses the closed-form trigonometric root of the cubic, $\sigma_k=p+2\sqrt{J_2/3}\cos(\phi-2\pi k/3)$ with $\phi=\tfrac13\arccos\big(3\sqrt3J_3/2J_2^{3/2}\big)$. The $\arccos$ argument is clamped to $[-1,1]$, and the hydrostatic test is relative to the stress scale so that small states are not misclassified.}

\textbf{Mohr's circle simulator.} Rotating the element about $z$ by $\theta$ moves the point $(\sigma_{x'},\tau_{x'y'})$ round a circle of radius $\sqrt{(\tfrac{\sigma_x-\sigma_y}{2})^2+\tau_{xy}^2}$ centred at $(\sigma_x+\sigma_y)/2$, at twice the rotation angle. \texttt{mohr.compute} in \file{sims.js} applies the transformation law, solves the 3D eigenproblem and draws all three circles; the safety factor is $\sigma_y/\sigma_{\text{eq}}$ (shown as $\infty$ for a hydrostatic state).

\section{Half-space elasticity}
\physics{A linear elastic body in equilibrium satisfies $\nabla\!\cdot\!\tens\sigma+\mathbf b=\mathbf 0$, $\tens\epsilon=\tfrac12(\nabla\mathbf u+\nabla\mathbf u^{T})$ and $\tens\sigma=\lambda\,\mathrm{tr}(\tens\epsilon)\mathbf I+2G\tens\epsilon$. These have closed-form solutions only for special geometries; the simplest useful one is the \emph{half-space} $z>0$ with a free surface. For a normal point force $P$ (Boussinesq~\cite{boussinesq,johnson}), at depth $z$ and radius $r$ with $R^2=r^2+z^2$,
\begin{align}
\sigma_z&=-\frac{3Pz^3}{2\pi R^5}, & \tau_{rz}&=-\frac{3Prz^2}{2\pi R^5},\\
\sigma_r&=\frac{P}{2\pi}\Big[\frac{1-2\nu}{R(R+z)}-\frac{3r^2z}{R^5}\Big], &
\sigma_\theta&=\frac{P(1-2\nu)}{2\pi}\Big[\frac{z}{R^3}-\frac{1}{R(R+z)}\Big].
\end{align}
The solution satisfies $\nabla\!\cdot\tens\sigma=\mathbf 0$, leaves the surface traction-free outside the load, and carries the full load through every horizontal plane, $\int_0^\infty\sigma_z\,2\pi r\,dr=-P$.}
\code{\texttt{boussinesqTensor} evaluates these in cylindrical form and rotates them to Cartesian axes. On the axis $\sigma_r=\sigma_\theta$, so the undefined azimuth does no harm.}

\textbf{Tangential load (Cerruti~\cite{cerruti}).} Its closed form is simplest as a displacement field. \texttt{tangentialTensor} differentiates that displacement numerically (central step $h=10^{-4}\sqrt{x^2+y^2+z_e^2}$) and applies Hooke's law, which guarantees a compatible strain. Since $\lambda\to\infty$ as $\nu\to0.5$, $\nu$ is capped at $0.499$ there.

\textbf{Equivalent depth.} A point load gives infinite stress beneath it, but a painted patch is a distributed pressure. The patch is represented by point loads at its mesh vertices, each shifted to a depth $z_e=z+a$, with $a$ chosen from the patch geometry so that neighbouring loads overlap smoothly. This is numerical smoothing, not a physical parameter.

\section{The 3D stress field pipeline}
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Loads.} A pressure $p$ on a patch of area $A$ is a force $P=pA$. Each vertex is given a third of the area of every mesh triangle it belongs to, so the force does not depend on how finely the mesh is sampled.
\item \textbf{Local frames.} Each load uses a frame with $\mathbf n_z=-\mathbf n_s$ (into the body); the load direction splits into a normal part (Boussinesq) and a tangential part (Cerruti).
\item \textbf{Rotation and superposition.} Each point-source tensor is rotated to global axes, $\tens\sigma_g=\mathbf R\tens\sigma_l\mathbf R^T$, and the \emph{tensors} from every point of every load are summed. Only then are principal stresses, von Mises stress and strain computed, because these are not linear in the tensor.
\item \textbf{Full-surface pressure.} When a normal pressure covers at least $99\%$ of the surface area, the half-space model is replaced by the exact hydrostatic state $\tens\sigma=-p\mathbf I$, with volumetric strain $-p/K$.
\item \textbf{Display.} Four maps are offered: normal stress $\max(|\sigma_1|,|\sigma_3|)$, maximum shear $(\sigma_1-\sigma_3)/2$, von Mises stress, and strain $\|\tens\epsilon\|$ from inverse Hooke's law. Displacements are the summed Boussinesq--Cerruti displacements, exaggerated by a user factor. Strains above $1\%$ are flagged as beyond small-strain theory.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Section analytics}
The 3D map shows only the surface; the drawer below it shows the stress on a plane cut through the body (Figure~\ref{fig:section}). \texttt{compute\_section} in \file{server.py} evaluates the same half-space tensors on a grid in the cut plane, vectorised with NumPy, takes all eigenvalues at once with \texttt{eigvalsh}, and blanks points outside the true shape (box, cylinder or sphere). The front end tags each request with a sequence number and ignores late replies, so dragging the slider never shows a stale picture.
