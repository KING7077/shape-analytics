\section{Approach}
A simulator can be wrong in ways that look reasonable. Each component was therefore compared with something independent: a closed form, a limiting case with a known answer, an invariant that must hold for any input, or a second implementation of the same physics. Table~\ref{tab:checks} summarises the checks recorded during development (the test scripts were later removed from the repository at the owner's request); Sections~\ref{sec:defaults} and~\ref{sec:convergence} were re-run for this report.

\begin{tabular}{@{}p{2.6cm}p{1.3cm}p{11.6cm}@{}}\toprule
Component & Checks & What was verified\\\midrule
\texttt{physics.js} & 54 & principal-stress invariants for 4000 random tensors over 12 decades; Boussinesq and Cerruti satisfy $\nabla\!\cdot\tens\sigma=\mathbf0$; $\int\sigma_z\,dA=-P$; free surface; far-field decay\\
\texttt{server.py} & 40 & eigenvalues against NumPy; $|\mathbf t|^2=\sigma_{nn}^2+\tau^2$; section linear in load and symmetric; browser and server agree to $2\times10^{-4}$\\

\section{Convergence of the finite element solver}
\label{sec:convergence}
\begin{figure}[ht]
\centering\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{../figures/fem_convergence.pdf}
\caption{FEM convergence. (a) Tapered bar tip displacement over exact. (b) Plane-stress cantilever tip deflection over Euler--Bernoulli, for Q4 and CST. (c) Stress concentration of a plate with a hole against plate half-width over hole radius; dashed line: Kirsch $K_t=3$.}\label{fig:conv}
\end{figure}
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item \textbf{Bar} (a): ratio $0.935$ with one element to $0.9999$ with thirty-two; the error falls about fourfold per mesh doubling, as expected for linear elements.
\item \textbf{Cantilever} (b): Q4 reaches $0.998$ with eight elements through the depth, CST only $0.954$; both are too stiff in bending on coarse meshes.
\item \textbf{Hole} (c): $K_t$ falls from $4.13$ for a plate three radii wide to $3.06$ for twenty, approaching Kirsch's value from above as the finite-width effect disappears.
\item \textbf{Block}: the centre stress is $1.26$, $1.06$ and $1.03$ times the half-space value for $2\times3$, $4\times4$ and $8\times8$~m blocks, so the 3D field's half-space model is good to about $3\%$ once the body is several times larger than the patch.
\end{itemize}

\section{Worked example: a simply supported beam}
A steel beam ($E=200$~GPa), section $80\times160$~mm, span $3$~m, central load $10$~kN. By hand, $I=bh^3/12=27.3\times10^{6}$~mm$^4$, so
\begin{equation*}
M_{\max}=\frac{PL}{4}=7.50\ \text{kN\,m},\qquad
\sigma_{\max}=\frac{M_{\max}c}{I}=21.97\ \text{MPa},\qquad
\delta_{\max}=\frac{PL^3}{48EI}=1.03\ \text{mm},
\end{equation*}
and the peak shear stress $1.5V/A=0.59$~MPa is 37 times smaller than the bending stress. The application gives exactly these values and a safety factor of $250/21.97=11.4$ (Figure~\ref{fig:beam}).
