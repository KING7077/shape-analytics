\label{ch:verify}

\section{Approach}
A simulator can be wrong in ways that look reasonable: a plausible picture with a wrong number. The checks here therefore compare the code with something independent: a closed-form result, a limiting case where the answer is known, an invariant that must hold for \emph{any} input, or a second implementation of the same physics. Where an exact answer is not available, a numerical experiment (mesh refinement, growing the body) shows the trend toward one.

\section{Defects found by the tests, and what was changed}
Each of the following was found by a limiting-case test and then corrected; the original behaviour is stated so that the reason for the code can be understood.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Torsion in the energy tab} (\file{sims.js}, \texttt{energy.compute}). The closed-form displacement and the work were $10^6$ too small because the torque was converted twice. The status check had a special case that skipped torsion, which hid the error. Both corrected.
\item \textbf{Short columns} (\texttt{buckling.compute}). Euler's critical stress was reported even above yield (up to $4\times10^4$~MPa) and the safety factor used it. A Johnson parabola now governs when $\lambda<\lambda_c$.
\item \textbf{Principal stresses} (\file{physics.js}, \texttt{principalStresses}; \file{sims.js}, \texttt{eig3}). A fixed threshold on $J_2$ treated small non-hydrostatic stress states as hydrostatic. The threshold is now relative to the stress scale.
\item \textbf{$\nu=0.5$} (\texttt{tangentialTensor} and \file{server.py}). The Lam\'e constant divides by $1-2\nu$: infinite stress in the browser and a division-by-zero error on the server. The value is capped at $0.499$ in that formula.
\item \textbf{Section endpoint}. A resolution below 2 crashed the request and a force direction that was not a unit vector scaled the load. Resolution is clamped (2 to 400) and the direction is normalised. Earlier in the project the section used the wrong axis for cylinders and drew stress outside the body; both were corrected.
\item \textbf{Display}. An infinite safety factor appeared as a dash and now shows $\infty$. Strains above $1\%$ carry a warning.
\item \textbf{Finite plate with a hole} (not a defect but a result): the first Kirsch comparison showed $K_t$ above $3$; the trend with the width ratio explains this.
\end{enumerate}

\chapter{Limitations and how to extend the project}
\label{ch:ext}

\section{Limits of validity}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Linear elastic and small strain} everywhere. Plasticity, large rotation, contact, dynamics and damage are not modelled. Rubber in the 3D tab can exceed $100\%$ strain; the numbers are then indicative only.
\item \textbf{Half-space} (3D tab, section): an infinite body. For a finite one it leaves stress on the sides and base. The FEM block quantifies this and shows the model is good for a deep, wide body. A \emph{partial} patch is not self-equilibrated in the 3D tab (there are no supports), and displacements are measured from the bottom centre.
\item \textbf{Beams and columns}: numerical integration on 401 points; constant shear factor; ideal straight columns. The Mohr tool accepts symmetric stress matrices only.
\item \textbf{FEM}: plane stress only (no plane strain or axisymmetric element); CST and Q4 are stiff in bending until refined; bricks lock for nearly incompressible materials; the dense 2D solver is capped near 3600 degrees of freedom and the 3D block near 9000 nodes; surface stress values are extrapolated; the block is a cuboid with a square patch aligned to the mesh.
\item \textbf{Environment}: Three.js, Plotly and the fonts come from CDNs, so the interface needs network access.
\end{itemize}

\section{Adding a simulator}
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
\item In \file{sims.js} define an object with the fields shown in Chapter~\ref{ch:sims}; the physics goes in \texttt{compute(v)}, returning readouts, charts and equations in the stated format.
\item Add it to the \texttt{SIMS} array; the navigation, controls, A/B comparison, exports and routing follow automatically.
\item Check it against at least two closed forms and the limits of every input (zero, minimum, maximum), since the safety factors and ratios divide by quantities that can be zero.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Removing the FEM tab}
It is self-contained: delete \file{fem.js}, \file{fem.css}, \file{fem.py} and \file{fem3d.py}; remove the \texttt{fem.css} link and the \texttt{fem.js} script tag from \file{index.html}; remove the two \texttt{fem\_router} lines from \file{server.py}; and remove the \texttt{registerModule} function in \file{sims.js}. Nothing else depends on it. \texttt{docs/FEM\_PLAN.md} describes the design and the staged scope.

\section{Ideas for further work}
Plane-strain and axisymmetric elements (to compare with the Lam\'e vessel); thermal loads in the FEM; a finite-element field for cylinders and spheres (needs a mesher); a unit switch; and wiring the FEM block result into the 3D tab as an alternative to the half-space solution.
